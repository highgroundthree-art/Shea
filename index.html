Cleaning-supply safety

Clean spaces shouldn't put the people you care for at risk.

For healthcare teams, daycares, home care providers and foster homes: clear, practical guidance and safer product choices, so the products that keep a space clean aren't the ones that cause harm.

Why cleaning-supply safety matters

Small bodies, bigger risk

Young children and frail adults are more sensitive to fumes, spills and accidental swallowing than a healthy adult is.

Mixing mistakes happen

Some common products release dangerous gases when combined. Busy shifts and shared supply closets make mix-ups easy.

Look-alike containers

Decanted bottles, unlabeled sprays and colorful packaging can be mistaken for drinks or for each other.

Built around how you actually work

Every setting has different risks. Choose yours.

Healthcare professionals

Clinics, facilities and care teams

  • Keep disinfectant contact times and dilution instructions visible at the point of use.
  • Standardize products across units so staff aren't improvising.
  • Keep Safety Data Sheets (SDS) current and easy to find, and train new hires on them.
  • Protect patients with respiratory sensitivities by ventilating after strong-smelling products.
Daycares

Centers and in-home child care

  • Store every product locked and out of sight and reach, never under a sink.
  • Clean when children are elsewhere, and let surfaces dry before they return.
  • Keep products in original containers with the original label.
  • Use the right product for the job: sanitize food surfaces, disinfect other surfaces, and follow the label for each.
Home care providers

Caregivers working in clients' homes

  • Ask about allergies, asthma and sensitivities before you bring products into a home.
  • Don't switch products mid-task or "boost" one with another.
  • Check that products in a client's home are labeled, not expired and not stored near food or medicine.
  • Carry a small, labeled kit instead of a bag of mixed bottles.
Foster care providers

Foster parents and placement support

  • Walk through the home with each new placement in mind: a child's age changes what is within reach.
  • Lock up cleaning products, laundry pods, dishwasher packs and air fresheners.
  • Plan for children who arrive with little warning, with a ready, child-safe storage spot.
  • Post the Poison Help number where every caregiver can see it.

Seven rules that prevent most cleaning-product accidents

  1. Never mix products. In particular, never combine bleach with ammonia or with acidic products such as some toilet-bowl and rust cleaners.
  2. Keep the original container and label. If you must transfer a product, label the new container clearly.
  3. Lock it up. Out of sight and out of reach is not enough; locked is better.
  4. Read the label every time. Dilution, contact time and ventilation notes matter.
  5. Ventilate. Open a window or run a fan, and keep vulnerable people out of the area.
  6. Wear the right protection. Gloves and eye protection when the label says so.
  7. Know your emergency step. In the U.S., Poison Help is 1-800-222-1222. For a medical emergency, call 911.

Important: This page is general safety education, not medical advice or a substitute for product labels, Safety Data Sheets, your facility's policies or licensing rules.
